% Abstract
\begin{abstract}
Large Language Models (LLMs) are increasingly used in diverse applications, but concerns about bias challenge their reliability. Among different possible biases, this thesis investigates ideological bias, focusing on how models vary across topics, roles, and languages. Two models—GPT 4o-mini and LLaMA 3.1-70B —were evaluated under conditions where they answered as specific national roles, as a neutral assistant, or in native languages. Bias was assessed through stance alignment, measuring agreement ratios, and through semantic similarity of justifications, capturing how consistently models explained their answers. Results show systematic divergences across conditions: agreement ratios varied by topic, with multiparty systems and economic sanctions showing the widest differences. Role assignments exerted stronger influence than language, with roles associated with the USA and European countries displaying higher alignment than those of Iran, Israel, and China. Assistant-role completions also diverged from role-based answers, further highlighting ideological variation. These findings contribute a two-level framework for assessing bias in LLMs and provide insights into their ideological behavior and societal implications.
\end{abstract}
